1. Introduction

Adventuity (Pty) Ltd was appointed by Sample client to determine the 1:50 and 1:100 year flood lines of the watercourse adjacent to Erf 1024 Demo Spruit. Flood lines are required under section 144 of the National Water Act (Act 36 of 1998), which prohibits the establishment of a township unless the layout plan shows the 1:100 year flood line, and by local authority by-laws that restrict development within floodplains.

The study consisted of (i) delineating the catchment from a digital terrain model, (ii) estimating design floods with the deterministic and empirical methods of the SANRAL Drainage Manual, (iii) computing water surface profiles with a one-dimensional steady-flow standard-step model equivalent to HEC-RAS, (iv) mapping the flood lines from the computed water levels, and (v) checking the result with a two-dimensional dynamic flood model.

2. Site and data

The site lies at latitude -26.06283°, longitude 28.88997° (WGS84), which is Y = 11 010.0 m, X = 2 883 800.0 m in the Hartebeesthoek94 Lo29 system. The study reach extends 1521 m along the watercourse, from 900 m upstream to 650 m downstream of the point nearest the site.

3. Catchment characteristics

The terrain model was conditioned with the Priority-Flood depression-filling algorithm (Barnes et al., 2014), after which D8 flow directions (O'Callaghan and Mark, 1984) and flow accumulation were computed. The catchment was delineated upstream of the downstream limit of the study reach, so the design flood applies conservatively to the full reach. The longest watercourse was traced from the outlet to the most remote point on the watershed.

4. Design flood hydrology

4.1 Time of concentration

For a defined watercourse the time of concentration follows the US Bureau of Reclamation equation recommended in the Drainage Manual:

Tc=(0.87L21000S)0.385(3)

with Tc in hours, L in km and S in m/m. Overland flow time was not added because the catchment drains through a defined watercourse.

Result: Tc = 1.33 h (80 min).

4.2 Rational and Alternative Rational methods

Q=C⋅I⋅A3.6(1)

Q is the peak flow (m³/s), C the runoff coefficient, I the average rainfall intensity over Tc (mm/h) and A the area (km²). The runoff coefficient combines rural, urban and lake components:

CT=αFtC1+βC2+γC3,C1=Cs+Cp+Cv(2)

α, β and γ are the rural, urban and lake fractions of the catchment (α = 1.00, β = 0.00, γ = 0.00). Cs, Cp and Cv are the surface slope, permeability and vegetation components for the MAP class (MAP 600–900 mm). Ft is the return-period adjustment for steep and impermeable catchments.

Point rainfall for the Rational Method follows the HRU 2/78 depth-duration-frequency relationship (Midgley and Pitman, 1978) as applied in the SANRAL design programs, for summer/inland rainfall:

PT=217.8(1+4.164Tc)0.8832Tc⋅18.79+0.17MAP100⋅FT(7)

The Alternative Rational Method uses the modified Hershfield equation (Alexander, 2001) for durations up to 6 hours:

Pt,T=1.13(0.41+0.64lnT)(−0.11+0.27lnt)(0.79M0.69R0.20)(8)

t is the storm duration (min, taken as Tc), T the return period (years), M the mean of the annual daily maximum rainfall (mm) and R the average number of thunder days per year. Point rainfall is reduced to areal rainfall with the areal reduction factor (%):

ARF=(90000−12800lnA+9830ln(60Tc))0.4(6)
IT=PT⋅ARF100Tc(9)

4.3 Standard Design Flood (SDF)

The SDF method (Alexander, 2002) calibrates the runoff coefficient to the drainage basin through the 2-year and 100-year coefficients C2 = 10% and C100 = 40%, with YT the standard normal variate of the return period:

CT=C2100+YT2.33(C100100−C2100),QT=0.278CTITA(10)

The Regional Maximum Flood of Kovács (1988) gives an upper envelope for the region (K = 4.6):

QRMF=c⋅Aen(11)

Result: QRMF = 275.5 m³/s (transition zone, 100·Ae^0.38).

5. Hydraulic analysis

5.1 Method

Water surface profiles were computed for steady, gradually varied flow with the standard step method, using the formulation of the HEC-RAS Hydraulic Reference Manual (USACE). Between adjacent cross-sections the energy equation is solved iteratively for the upstream water surface:

WS2+α2V222g=WS1+α1V122g+he(12)
he=LS‾f+C|α2V222g−α1V122g|(13)

WS is the water surface elevation, V the mean velocity, α the velocity weighting coefficient, he the energy loss, L the discharge-weighted reach length and C the contraction (0.1) or expansion (0.3) coefficient. Conveyance is computed separately for the left overbank, the main channel and the right overbank, and the friction slope uses the average-conveyance form:

K=1nAR23,S‾f=(Q1+Q2K1+K2)2(14)
L=LlobQ‾lob+LchQ‾ch+LrobQ‾robQ‾lob+Q‾ch+Q‾rob(15)
α=At2∑Ki3Ai2Kt3(16)

Where the energy balance has no subcritical solution, the water surface is set to critical depth, found as the minimum of the specific energy:

E=WS+αV22gis a minimum at critical depth(17)

5.3 Hydraulic structures

Each structure is an internal boundary between a cross-section just downstream and one just upstream of it, with ineffective flow areas outside the opening. For bridges in low flow the energy equation is solved through the opening (contraction 0.3, expansion 0.5) and, where piers exist, Yarnell's equation is also applied; the higher upstream level governs:

Δy=2K(K+10ω−0.6)(α+15α4)V322g(20)

K is the pier shape coefficient, α the ratio of pier area to unobstructed area, ω the downstream velocity head divided by depth and V₃ the downstream velocity. When the energy line reaches the soffit the bridge runs under pressure, as a sluice gate when only the upstream face is submerged (21) or as an orifice when both faces are submerged (22), and flow over the road follows the broad-crested weir equation with Villemonte's submergence factor (23):

Q=CdABU2g(Y3−Z2+α3V322g)(21)
Q=CA2gH(22)
Q=CwLH1.5⋅(1−(hdH)1.5)0.385(23)

Culverts follow FHWA HDS-5 (2012). Inlet control uses the unsubmerged (24) and submerged (25) forms with Ku = 1.811 in SI units; outlet control uses the full-barrel energy equation (26). The higher headwater governs, and flow above the road crest is split between the culvert and weir flow over the road.

6. Dynamic two-dimensional check

To test the steady one-dimensional result, the 1:100 year flood hydrograph was routed through the reach with a two-dimensional dynamic model based on the local inertial form of the shallow water equations (Bates et al., 2010), the formulation used in LISFLOOD-FP. Unit discharges between cells are updated explicitly:

qt+Δt=qt−ghfΔt∂η∂x1+gΔtn2|qt|/hf7/3(18)
Δt=0.7Δxghmax(19)

q is the unit discharge (m²/s), hf the flow depth between cells, η the water surface elevation and n the Manning coefficient. The time step satisfies the Courant condition (19). The inflow hydrograph has an SCS-type gamma shape with time to peak 0.67 Tc and the selected peak discharge; outflow leaves the downstream cross-section at normal depth.

7. Flood lines

The water surface was interpolated between adjacent cross-sections in proportion to distance, intersected with the terrain model, and limited to areas hydraulically connected to the channel, as in HEC-RAS RAS Mapper. The 1:50 and 1:100 year flood lines are the edges of the resulting inundated areas.

8. Limitations and recommendations

  • The accuracy of flood lines depends mainly on the terrain model. This demonstration uses synthetic terrain and must not be used for any real site.
  • Structures were modelled from the dimensions given; confirm them on site. Debris blockage was taken as stated in Table 9 and should be checked against the catchment’s debris potential.
  • Manning coefficients were estimated from typical values; a site inspection should confirm channel and floodplain roughness.
  • The flood lines reflect present catchment conditions. Future urbanisation, channel changes or dam failure are not included.
  • A freeboard or buffer beyond the 1:100 year flood line should be applied in accordance with the requirements of the local authority.
  • The design floods should be compared with recorded floods at nearby DWS flow gauging stations where these are available.
