Municipal approvals

Flood lines for building plans and site development plans

Municipalities restrict building in the floodplain and often ask for the 1:50 and 1:100 year flood lines before they approve a site development plan or building plan near a river, spruit or stream.

Why the municipality asks

  • Buildings inside the flood line are at risk and can make flooding worse for neighbours.
  • Stormwater and environmental by-laws require proof that the building platform is above the flood level.
  • Environmental authorisation is often needed for development within 32 m of a watercourse; the flood line helps define the setback.

What you receive

  • Signed report and A3 drawings showing the flood lines on your stand.
  • Flood levels so your architect can set the floor and platform levels.
  • CAD files for the architect and land surveyor.
Questions

Flood lines explained

My stand floods only near the stream. Can I still build?

Usually yes, on the part of the stand outside the flood line. The study shows exactly where that is and how high the floor must be.

Do I need a land survey?

For approval, the flood line must be based on a topographical survey or LiDAR. We can use your surveyor’s data or arrange the survey for you.

What is the 1:100 year flood line?

It is the edge of the area flooded by a flood that has a 1% chance of being equalled or exceeded in any year. It does not mean the flood happens once every hundred years; two can occur in the same decade.

How long does a flood line study take?

Usually five to ten working days after the survey data is available. Urgent work can be arranged.

Who signs the report?

The flood lines are certified by a professional engineer registered with the Engineering Council of South Africa (ECSA).
