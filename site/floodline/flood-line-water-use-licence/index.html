Water use and environmental approval

Flood lines for water use licences and environmental applications

For section 21(c) and (i) water uses, the regulated area of a watercourse is measured to the outer edge of the 1:100 year flood line or the riparian habitat, whichever is greater. Environmental practitioners and specialists use our flood lines to set buffers and assess impacts.

Questions

Flood lines explained

Is the flood line the same as the wetland or riparian boundary?

No. They are different lines. The regulated area uses whichever is further from the watercourse, so most applications need both.

Which return periods do you map?

Normally the 1:50 and 1:100 year flood lines. We can add the 1:20 year line or the regional maximum flood if the authority asks.

What is the 1:100 year flood line?

It is the edge of the area flooded by a flood that has a 1% chance of being equalled or exceeded in any year. It does not mean the flood happens once every hundred years; two can occur in the same decade.

How long does a flood line study take?

Usually five to ten working days after the survey data is available. Urgent work can be arranged.

Who signs the report?

The flood lines are certified by a professional engineer registered with the Engineering Council of South Africa (ECSA).
