Planning and building approval

Flood risk assessments for development sites

Planning authorities around the world ask for a flood risk assessment before they approve development near a river or stream. We map the design floods on your site and deliver a signed report with drawings your architect and planner can use.

What the assessment answers

  • Where the 1:50, 1:100 and 1:200 year floods reach on your site.
  • How deep the water is at buildings, roads and access points.
  • Which part of the site can be developed safely, and at what floor level.

What you receive

  • Signed flood risk report with the results and flood levels at the site.
  • Flood lines on a satellite photo and on contours, with your property boundary.
  • CAD and GIS files in your local coordinate system, and Google Earth files.
Questions

Flood studies explained

Do you follow our local flood study rules?

Yes. Tell us the country and the approving authority; we use the official design rainfall for the site and work to the authority's requirements.

Can you work without a survey?

For a desktop assessment and for quoting we use satellite terrain. Approval flood lines need a topographical survey or LiDAR, which your surveyor can supply or we can arrange.

What is the 1:100 year flood line?

It is the edge of the area flooded by a flood that has a 1% chance of being equalled or exceeded in any year. It does not mean the flood happens once every hundred years; two can occur in the same decade.

How long does a flood study take?

Usually five to ten working days after the survey data is available. Urgent work can be arranged.

Who signs the report?

A registered professional engineer certifies the flood lines, with the registration your approving authority asks for.

How do I pay?

You receive a fixed quotation. Once you accept it, you can pay online by card through a secure payment link.
