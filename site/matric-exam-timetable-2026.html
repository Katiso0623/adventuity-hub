What to do in the last two weeks

  1. Write full papers under exam conditions

    Sit a whole past paper in one go: 3 hours, 150 marks, no notes and no phone. That is 1,2 minutes per mark, so a 10-mark question deserves about 12 minutes – practise moving on when you go over.

  2. Mark yourself with the official memo

    Mark strictly, the way an examiner would, and keep an error list. Two days later, redo every question where you lost marks without looking at the memo.

  3. Spend most time where the marks are

    Trigonometry is worth roughly 50 marks out of 150 in Mathematics Paper 2, and mechanics roughly 65 in Physics. Revise the heavy topics first, but do not skip a whole question.

  4. Know your formula and data sheets

    Learn exactly which formulae and constants are given in the exam and which you must remember, and what every symbol stands for, so you do not waste time searching.

  5. Show every step

    Start with the formula, put in the values and give the answer with its unit – method marks add up even if the final answer is wrong. In Euclidean geometry, back each statement with a reason.

  6. Plan the paper on the day

    Read the whole paper first, start with a question you are sure of, number your answers exactly as in the paper and never leave a blank – an attempt can still earn marks.

  7. Check your calculator

    Use an approved non-programmable calculator with a fresh battery, and make sure it is set to degrees before any trigonometry.

  8. Make the day before a light one

    Go over your summaries and error list instead of starting new work. Pack your admission letter, ID, pens, pencil, ruler and calculator, and get a full night’s sleep.
