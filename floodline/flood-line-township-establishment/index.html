National Water Act, section 144

Flood lines for township establishment

No township may be established unless its layout plan shows the 1:100 year flood line. We deliver the certified flood line, the report and CAD files your town planner needs for the township application.

When it applies

  • Section 144 of the National Water Act requires the 1:100 year flood line on the layout plan of every new township.
  • Rezoning, subdivision and township extension applications next to a watercourse are usually asked for the same.
  • The flood line also shows which erven can be developed and where open space or servitudes are needed.

What your planner receives

  • Signed flood line report for the application.
  • 1:50 and 1:100 year flood lines in Lo coordinates as DXF, ready to drop onto the layout.
  • A3 drawings with the property boundary, flood lines and flood levels.
Questions

Flood lines explained

Does the flood line have to be on the layout plan?

Yes. Section 144 of the National Water Act states that no person may establish a township unless the layout plan shows the 1:100 year flood line.

Can the layout be changed after the flood line is known?

Yes, that is the purpose. The flood line shows which part of the land is safe to develop, and the planner adjusts the erven, roads and open space to suit.

What is the 1:100 year flood line?

It is the edge of the area flooded by a flood that has a 1% chance of being equalled or exceeded in any year. It does not mean the flood happens once every hundred years; two can occur in the same decade.

How long does a flood line study take?

Usually five to ten working days after the survey data is available. Urgent work can be arranged.

Who signs the report?

The flood lines are certified by a professional engineer registered with the Engineering Council of South Africa (ECSA).
